# music-store-sales-analysis
Music Store Data Analysis using PostgreSQL. Queried customer spending, top genres by country, sales revenue, and artist track counts via multi-table joins, subqueries, CTEs, and window functions (RANK) to uncover actionable business insights.


Music Store Data Analysis (PostgreSQL)Project OverviewThis project analyzes a digital music store's relational database using PostgreSQL to uncover insights into customer purchasing behavior, sales revenue trends, artist popularity, and regional genre preferences.Key Business Questions AnsweredEmployee & Organizational Insights: Identified the senior-most employee based on job hierarchy levels.Revenue & Market Performance: Determined countries with the highest invoice counts, top invoice totals, and the highest-grossing city for hosting a promotional music festival.Customer Spending Analysis: Identified the top overall spending customer and calculated exact expenditure per customer across individual artists.Music & Genre Trends: Extracted all Rock music listeners, ranked the top 10 Rock bands by track count, filtered tracks exceeding average song duration, and determined the most popular music genre per country (including tied rankings).   SQL Techniques & Skills DemonstratedMulti-Table Joins: Connected normalized tables (customer, invoice, invoice_line, track, album, artist, genre) to synthesize cross-entity metrics.Aggregations & Filtering: Used SUM(), COUNT(), AVG(), ROUND(), GROUP BY, HAVING, and DISTINCT for accurate metric calculations and deduplication.Subqueries: Implemented scalar subqueries to dynamically filter tracks longer than the dataset's average duration.CTEs & Window Functions: Leveraged Common Table Expressions (WITH) alongside RANK() OVER(PARTITION BY ...) to handle regional genre ties cleanly.
